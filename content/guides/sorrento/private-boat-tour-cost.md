## What a private boat costs

Public listings for a private boat with a skipper from Sorrento sit in a wide band. A full day to Capri of seven to eight hours runs from about €800 to €1,200 per boat, and one listing for the longer run to Positano and Amalfi starts at €1,400. A half day of four hours starts around €500. The few sunset listings we found, for two hours, run from €400 to €900.

That band is real, and so is the reason for it. The price is for the boat, and boats differ a lot: a small speedboat for four people and a twelve-metre gozzo with a cabin and a toilet are both called "private boat tour".

Two warnings about the numbers. Most local operators hide their prices in November and publish new ones before the season, so several figures below are "from" prices for a date in March 2027. July and August cost more. We could not verify by how much, so we are not going to guess.

BlueKeys, a charter company in Sorrento, puts a full day on a 7 to 8 metre gozzo at €800 to €1,100 in its 2026 price guide, and a half day from about €550.

## What is in the price and what is not

A skipper is included in every listing we read. So are drinks of some kind, towels on most boats, and insurance. Lunch is never included.

Fuel is the one to watch. It does not depend on whether you go to Capri or the Amalfi Coast. It depends on the operator. Gianni's Boat, Grassi Junior, Buyourtour and Charter System include it. Sorrento Sea Tours charges €300 on top, and MBS Blu Charter €250.

| Extra | Typical amount | When you pay |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel, where not included | €250–300 per boat | On the day |
| Blue Grotto ticket and rowboat | about €18–19 per person | Cash, at the grotto |
| Docking at Marina Grande, Capri | per boat, if you go ashore | On the day |
| Boarding fee in Sorrento | €20 per boat on one listing, €10 per person on another | At the meeting point |
| Hotel pick-up | included, or €7–15 per person | On the day |
| Lunch ashore | not included anywhere | At the restaurant |

On the Capri docking fee: listings mention figures around €100 to €150 per boat, but we could not confirm an official tariff, so treat that as a rough guide. On the Blue Grotto: listings quote anything from €14 to €20. Most sources agree on about €18 to €19, paid in cash at the floating ticket office.

## What changes the price

### The boat

Size sets the price more than anything else. A four-person speedboat is listed from €500. A gozzo for twelve starts near €950. An operator's own advice is worth repeating: Grassi Junior suggests its larger Aprea 32 or 36 gozzo boats because they are steadier at sea and less likely to be cancelled.

### The hours

A half day is four hours on the water. A full day adds time ashore: seven-hour tours dock at Marina Grande, Capri's harbour, so you can go up to the Piazzetta.

### The month

The season runs roughly from late March to the end of October, with some operators sailing until mid-November. July and August are the busiest months and operators ask you to book well ahead, weekends above all.

### Where you start

Most boats leave from [Marina Piccola](/amalfi-coast/sorrento/guides/marina-piccola-sorrento), Sorrento's port. Some operators also board at Piano di Sorrento and Massa Lubrense, where one offers free parking.

Take the gozzo listed from €950 with fuel included and split it. Remember it is a "from" price for a date in March.

Shared day tours to Capri from Sorrento are listed from €114 to €158 per person for the 2027 season. So the crossover sits between six and eight people. At six, the private boat matches the dearest shared tour. At eight or nine it matches the cheapest, and you choose the stops. For two people a shared boat is a quarter to a third of the price. If you only need to get across, the ferry is far cheaper: see [Sorrento to Capri by boat](/amalfi-coast/sorrento/guides/sorrento-to-capri-by-boat).

## Five questions to ask before you book

1. Is fuel in the price? If not, how much is it?
2. Which fees do I pay in cash on the day, and how much is each?
3. What is the maximum number of people on this boat?
4. Do we land on Capri, and who pays the docking fee?
5. If the sea is rough, do I get a new date or my money back?

A reviewer on one listing, in October 2026, wrote about being charged more than €50 per person in fees at the end of the day that nobody had explained. That is the situation these questions avoid.
