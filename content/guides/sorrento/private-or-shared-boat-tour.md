## The difference in one table

| | Small-group boat | Private boat |
|---|---|---|
| You pay | Per person | Per boat |
| Listed price, day to Capri | from €114–158 per person | from about €800–1,200 per boat |
| People on board | Up to 12 strangers and you | Your group only, 4 to 12 depending on the boat |
| Route and stops | Set by the operator | Agreed with the skipper |
| Time ashore on Capri | 3–4 hours, fixed | As long as you like within the hours booked |
| Blue Grotto | Sometimes, if the group agrees to queue | Your call |
| Departure | In the morning, usually from Marina Piccola | When you choose |
| Cash on the day | €5–10 per person "destination fee" | Fuel if not included, docking if you land |

Prices are "from" figures published by Sorrento operators for the 2027 season, checked on 14 November 2026.

## When a shared boat is the better choice

Two people, a normal budget, first time on this coast. That is the case for a shared boat, and it is a good day out. You get the same water and the same swim stops as the private boats next to you.

The listings are consistent on what a shared day includes: seven to eight hours, a gozzo with a sundeck, drinks and something to eat, fuel in the price. Sorrento Sea Tours hands out a caprese sandwich. Buyourtour adds a prosecco and limoncello tasting and picks you up from your hotel.

What you give up is control. The boat leaves when it leaves, and the three or four hours on Capri are the same for everybody.

## When a private boat costs less per head

The maths is short. A twelve-seat gozzo with fuel included is listed from €950 for the day. Divide by six and you get €158 each, which is the top of the shared range. Divide by eight and it is €119, close to the cheapest shared tour at €114.

So a family of six, or two couples travelling with their parents, pays about what the dearer shared tours cost. A group of eight pays about what the cheapest costs. At that point the private boat is the better product: nobody else on board, lunch where you want it, and a route you set with the skipper.

There is a second case, and it has nothing to do with arithmetic. Small children, a grandparent who is unsure on a ladder, or somebody who gets seasick: all of them are easier on a boat that can turn back or slow down without asking eleven other people.

Full numbers are in [how much a private boat from Sorrento really costs](/amalfi-coast/sorrento/guides/private-boat-tour-cost).

## What "small group" really means

"Small group" is not a protected term. On this coast it usually means up to 12 passengers on a wooden gozzo, and that is what most Sorrento listings state. One Amalfi Coast tour sets its limit at 8 and charges accordingly: from €266 per person. A semi-private half day from Piano di Sorrento allows 14.

Some listings do not give the number at all. Ask, and ask how long the boat is.

## The fees that are not in the ticket

Shared tours nearly all add a payment in cash on the day. Operators call it a destination fee, a docking fee or a city tax, and it is €5 or €10 per person depending on the company. The Blue Grotto, where the stop is offered, is about €18 to €19 per person on top, also in cash. Hotel pick-up is free with some operators and €7 to €15 per person with others.

None of this is hidden exactly. It is in the small print of each listing. But it means the price you compare should be the ticket plus the fees.

## Questions to ask before you book

1. What is the maximum number of people on board?
2. How long do we have ashore, and where?
3. Which fees are paid in cash on the day?
4. Is the Blue Grotto a planned stop or only if conditions allow?
5. Where exactly do we meet, and is hotel pick-up included?
