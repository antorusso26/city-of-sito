## What Marina Grande is

Marina Grande is Sorrento's fishing village. Today it is known for two things: a row of restaurants on the water and a small beach.

It is not the port. Ferries leave from [Marina Piccola](/amalfi-coast/sorrento/guides/marina-piccola-sorrento). And it is not the Marina Grande on your ferry ticket either: that one is on Capri.

## How to walk there

From Piazza della Vittoria, take the lane that runs beside the Hotel Bellevue Syrene. Follow it until it turns into steps. Partway down you walk under a stone arch.

That arch is the Greek gate, the Porta di Marina Grande. Most sources date it to the 4th century BC, which makes it the oldest piece of Greek building left in Sorrento. It was later built into the 16th-century town walls, and it was probably once the only way into the town from the sea.

A few more steps and you are on the waterfront.

## Eating by the water

This is the reason most people come. The restaurants sit along the water, and seafood is what they do. Two names come up in every guidebook: Trattoria Da Emilia, family-run since 1947, and Delfino, which has a bathing deck as well as a dining room. Check that they are open before you walk down, and book ahead in summer.

## The beach

The beach is small, of volcanic sand, and rocky in places. Sunbeds and umbrellas are for hire.

## Getting back up

For the climb back, the usual advice is to take the bus. A minibus links Piazza Tasso and Marina Grande every 30 minutes between 07:15 and 19:30, for €1.20. Check the time of the last one before you sit down to dinner. After that it is the steps or a taxi.

## While you are here
