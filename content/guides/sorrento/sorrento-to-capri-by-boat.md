## Three ways to get there

| | Ferry or hydrofoil | Small-group boat tour | Private boat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | about €21–28 one way | from €114–158 per person | from about €800 per boat |
| Time on the water | 20–30 minutes each way | 7–8 hours, with stops | 4 to 8 hours, your choice |
| Leaves from | Marina Piccola | Marina Piccola, in the morning | Marina Piccola, Piano di Sorrento or Massa Lubrense |
| Swim stops | No | Yes | Yes |
| Time on Capri | As long as you like | 3–4 hours | You decide |

Fares and tour prices as published on 14 November 2026. Tour prices are "from" figures for the 2027 season.

## By ferry

The ferry is the cheapest and fastest way across. Four companies run the route: NLG, Caremar, Gescab and Laser Capri. The high-speed boats take 20 minutes. Caremar's fast ferry takes 30 and is the cheapest, at €21.40 one way. The others charge €24 to €26, and €28 on one late-afternoon run.

### Where it leaves and where it lands

Every company sails from [Marina Piccola](/amalfi-coast/sorrento/guides/marina-piccola-sorrento), Sorrento's port, below the town. From Piazza Tasso it is about ten minutes down the steps, or you take the lift from the Villa Comunale gardens, or a bus. You land at Marina Grande, Capri's main harbour. The funicular from there to the Piazzetta takes four or five minutes.

### How often

On the timetable for mid-November 2026 there are about 13 departures a day, the last at 19:25. More boats run from April to October. Some departures are weekdays only, others weekends only, so check the day you travel.

### Tickets and luggage

Buy online or at the ticket offices in the port. In July and August, book ahead. Each company has its own luggage rule: Caremar carries a trolley of up to 20 kg free, while NLG charges about €3 for a small extra bag and €7 for a large one. Only Caremar takes vehicles, and visitors cannot bring a car to Capri between April and October anyway.

### What the ferry does not show you

The ferry goes port to port. There are no swim stops and no time at sea beyond the crossing, and the Blue Grotto is a separate trip once you land. If the water is the point of the day, you want a boat tour.

## On a small-group boat tour

A shared boat from Sorrento sails to Capri, stops for a swim, and leaves you ashore for three or four hours before sailing back. Listed prices for 2027 start at €114 with Buyourtour and €158 with Acampora Travel. Groups are up to 12.

Add the cash fee paid on the day, €5 or €10 per person depending on the operator. Lunch on the island is yours to pay.

Compare that with the ferry. Going by the one-way fares, a return ticket is about €43 to €56 per person. The shared tour costs roughly €60 to €115 more, and for that you get a day on the water, the swim stops and drinks on board. If time at sea is why you came, the tour is the better use of the day.

## On a private boat

A private boat with a skipper starts at about €800 for a full day and €500 for a half day in public listings, and takes between 4 and 12 people depending on the boat. You pick the departure time, the stops, where to eat and how long to stay.

Whether fuel is in the price varies by operator, and it can add €250 to €300. We go through every cost in [how much a private boat from Sorrento really costs](/amalfi-coast/sorrento/guides/private-boat-tour-cost).

## The Blue Grotto, whichever way you go

The Blue Grotto is not included in any ferry ticket or tour price. You pay at a floating ticket office at the cave mouth, in cash, about €18 to €19 per person, then step into a rowboat that takes you in. Listings quote anything from €14 to €20, so carry a little more than you think you need.

It also closes often, whenever the sea is rough. It is frequently shut between November and early March, and it can close on a summer day too: a review from 21 September 2026 mentions finding it closed. No tour can promise it.

## Which one for a single day on Capri

- **You want to walk Capri town and Anacapri.** Take the ferry. You get the most time on the island for the least money.
- **You want to see the island from the sea and still have lunch ashore.** Take a small-group tour.
- **You are six or more, or it is a special day.** Take a private boat. With eight people it costs about the same per head as a shared one.
- **The sea is rough.** Take the Caremar ferry, the last to stop running, or move the day.
